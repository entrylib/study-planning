CLAIMSET What does sMAPE reward? An analytical and simulation study
summary Which forecast does sMAPE reward? sMAPE has been criticised for its asymmetry and its behaviour near zero, but the forecast that minimises it had been found only in special cases. We show that for positive values sMAPE depends only on the ratio of actual to forecast, not on their difference: it is the hyperbolic tangent of half the absolute log error. So it scores a forecast k times too high and one k times too low the same, and is asymmetric only for equal absolute errors. Being bounded, it has a finite expected value for every distribution. Its target is a median of the actuals, each weighted by how close it is to the forecast by ratio. This is the median when the log of the actual is symmetric and unimodal; otherwise it can move to the side of the median that carries more weight. With two well-separated groups of actuals, the median becomes a local maximum. On intermittent demand the target is 0 once the share of zeros passes a threshold below one half. Otherwise sMAPE aims at a weighted median of the non-zero actuals. As the actuals spread out, expected sMAPE approaches its bound for every forecast, so forecasts become hard to tell apart, and the target moves from the median to the mode of the log. Where sMAPE and MAE both target the median, a test set's sMAPE minimiser estimates it less precisely than the sample median, increasingly so as the actuals spread out.

DEFINITIONS
D2 [variable] sMAPE: Symmetric mean absolute percentage error of a point forecast $f$ over a test set of actuals $y_1, \dots, y_n$: $\frac{200}{n}\sum_t |y_t - f|/(|y_t|+|f|)$, where a term with $y_t = f = 0$ counts as 0. Introduced as the symmetric MAPE by Makridakis (1993), whose denominator had no absolute values; for positive actuals and forecasts the two agree. [source B8]
D1 [variable] sMAPE term ($s(y,f)$): The contribution of one actual $y$ and one point forecast $f$ to [[D2:sMAPE]]: $s(y,f) = |y-f|/(|y|+|f|)$, between 0 and 1, with $s = 0$ when $y = f$ (including $y = f = 0$, where the fraction is $0/0$).
D3 [variable] expected sMAPE: The expected sMAPE of a fixed forecast $f$ under the distribution of the actual $y$: $200\,\mathrm{E}[s(y, f)]$, where $\mathrm E$ is the expectation over the random actual $y$. Computed from the distribution (by integration or exact sums), never from a sample.
D6 [concept] sMAPE target: The value or values of $f$ that minimise expected sMAPE for a given distribution of the actual $y$, $\arg\min_f \mathrm E[s(y,f)]$: the functional sMAPE rewards. It may be a single value or several.
D4 [variable] log error ($z$): For a positive actual $y$ and a positive forecast $f$, $z = \ln y - \ln f$.
D5 [variable] weight ($w(y,f)$): For a positive actual $y$ and a positive forecast $f$, $w(y,f) = y/(y+f)^2$: how much the actual pulls the forecast that minimises expected sMAPE, since the derivative of $\mathrm E[s(y,f)]$ in $f$ is $2\,\mathrm E[w(y,f)(\mathbf 1\{y<f\} - \mathbf 1\{y>f\})]$. Largest at $y = f$, the same for $y/f = r$ and $1/r$.
D7 [concept] quantile profile: For an outcome distribution with quantile function $Q$ and a forecast $f$, the pull of the outcome at each quantile level $u$, $u \mapsto w(Q(u), f)$ on $(0,1)$; the minimiser's quantile level balances its integrals below and above.
D10 [variable] RMSE: Root mean squared error of a point forecast $f$ over a test set of actuals $y_1, \dots, y_n$: $\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_t (y_t-f)^2}$.
D11 [variable] MAE: Mean absolute error of a point forecast $f$ over a test set of actuals $y_1, \dots, y_n$: $\frac1n\sum_t |y_t-f|$.
D12 [variable] MAPE: Mean absolute percentage error of a point forecast $f$ over a test set of actuals $y_1, \dots, y_n$: $\frac{100}{n}\sum_t |y_t-f|/|y_t|$; infinite when an actual is zero.
D19 [variable] log-MAE: 100 times the mean absolute error of $\ln f$ as a forecast of $\ln y$: $\frac{100}{n}\sum_t |\ln y_t - \ln f|$. Unbounded; sMAPE's small-error limit (C3); minimised by the median.
D28 [variable] Suilin term ($\tilde s(y,f)$): The contribution of one actual $y$ and one forecast $f$ to the Suilin loss:
$$\tilde s(y,f) = \frac{|y-f|}{\max(|y|+|f|+\delta,\ 0.5+\delta)}, \qquad \delta = 0.1;$$
the Suilin loss is 200 times its mean over a test set. It replaces the denominator $|y|+|f|$ of the [[D1:sMAPE term]]. Proposed by Suilin (2017) in code for a Kaggle competition, as a smoothed variant of sMAPE; defined in print by Hewamalage, Bergmeir and Bandara (2021, Eq. 15), who write the constant $\delta$ as $\epsilon$ and the factor 2 into the measure differently (a constant factor changes no property here). Hewamalage, Ackermann and Bergmeir (2023) call it msMAPE; Smyl (2025) gives that name to a different measure, so this study follows Kolassa (2026) and calls it the Suilin loss. Version 2 of this paper called it msMAPE. [source B14]
D29 [concept] Suilin target: The value or values of $f$ that minimise the expected [[D28:Suilin term]] for a given distribution of the actual: the functional the Suilin loss rewards, as the [[D6:sMAPE target]] is the one sMAPE rewards.
D33 [variable] msMAPE term ($s_\nu(y,f)$): The contribution of one actual $y \ge 0$ and one forecast $f \ge 0$ to the sparse-proof sMAPE (msMAPE) of Smyl (2025), as Kolassa (2026, Definition 4) states it, for a small constant $\nu > 0$ (Kolassa writes it $m$, which is the median here):
$$s_\nu(y,f) = \frac{|f-y|}{f+\max\{y, \nu\}},$$
which is 0 when $y = f = 0$; msMAPE is 200 times its mean over a test set (Kolassa writes the factor 2 into the term). It replaces the actual in the denominator of the [[D1:sMAPE term]] by $\nu$ when the actual is below $\nu$. [source B13]
D34 [concept] msMAPE target: The value or values of $f$ that minimise the expected [[D33:msMAPE term]] for a given distribution of the actual and a given constant $\nu$: the functional msMAPE rewards, as the [[D6:sMAPE target]] is the one sMAPE rewards.
D8 [variable] sMAPE minimiser of a test set ($\hat f_n$): The forecast that minimises the sMAPE of one test set $y_1, \dots, y_n$, $\hat f_n = \arg\min_f \frac1n\sum_t s(y_t, f)$, found numerically (the sample counterpart of the [[D6:sMAPE target]]).
D9 [variable] relative efficiency ($\mathrm{RE}(n)$): For a test-set size $n$ and an outcome distribution where sMAPE and MAE both target the median: $\mathrm{RE}(n) = \operatorname{Var}(\ln \hat m_n) / \operatorname{Var}(\ln \hat f_n)$ over test sets, where $\hat m_n$ is the sample median (the MAE minimiser) and $\hat f_n$ the sMAPE minimiser of the same test set. Above 1, sMAPE's minimiser varies less; below 1, MAE's does.
D20 [variable] ranking agreement: For one distribution, one measure, a test-set size $n$, the measure's target and a forecast at another quantile level: the probability that a test set of $n$ outcomes gives the target the strictly lower score (ties count as disagreement); 0.5 is a coin toss.
D32 [variable] signal-to-noise ratio of a comparison: For a measure with term $L(y,f)$, two forecasts $f_1$ and $f_2$ and a distribution of the actual $y$: $|\mathrm E[D]|/\mathrm{sd}(D)$, where $D = L(y,f_2) - L(y,f_1)$ is the difference of the two forecasts' scores on one actual. Over a test set of $n$ independent actuals the mean of $D$ is $\sqrt n$ times this many of its standard errors from zero: the larger the ratio, the smaller the test set that tells the two forecasts apart.
D13 [concept] candidate forecast: A point forecast fixed from the outcome distribution before any test set is drawn and the same for every test set: a quantile at a level from 0.01 to 0.99, the mean, the forecast 0, the log-mode, the minimiser of expected MAPE, or a local minimiser of expected sMAPE. It is never estimated from a sample.
D14 [concept] preferred candidate: Of the candidate forecasts for one distribution, the one a measure gives the lowest expected score, or, where every expected score is infinite but their differences are finite, the one the differences rank first (C8); none where no forecast is ranked first.
D15 [concept] derived value: A value computed from the outcome distribution itself by numerical integration of its density (Gauss-Legendre quadrature and a convolution on a grid of 524288 points of $\ln y$), never from a sample: the expected scores, the local minima of expected sMAPE, the formulas of the hypotheses.
D16 [method] selection design: For each outcome distribution: derive the candidate forecasts and their expected scores; then, for each test-set size $n$, draw test sets of $n$ independent outcomes, score every candidate by every measure on each test set, and count the candidate with the lowest score as the measure's choice (a tie of $j$ candidates counts $1/j$ each; a test set in which every candidate scores infinite chooses none).
D17 [method] efficiency design: For each outcome distribution and test-set size $n$: draw test sets of $n$ independent outcomes; in each, compute the sample median and the exact sMAPE minimiser (an outcome of the test set, since the test set's sMAPE is concave in $\ln f$ between consecutive outcomes); over test sets, compare the variances of their logarithms.
D18 [method] flatness design: For families whose spread $\sigma$ of $\ln y$ grows from 0.1 to 10: derive the sMAPE target, the expected sMAPE of forecasts at fixed quantile levels and the large-spread approximation of C10; on test sets, count how often each measure scores the target lower than a forecast away from it, and where the test set's sMAPE minimiser falls.
D31 [method] formula checks: Numerical checks of the formulas of the formal claims (claimset-code/smape-target/check_formulas.py, seed 6; check_jump.py for C11): identities and inequalities on 200000 random pairs, derivatives against finite differences, minimisers, balances and thresholds against quadrature and bisection on every setting of the selection design, and the large-spread expansion against its bound. For the claims new or reworded in version 3 (C3, C4, C12, C17 to C20): check_v3.py (seed 8), check_h39.py and check_corrected.py (seed 8), in the same way.
D21 [population] inverse Gaussian outcomes: Inverse Gaussian outcomes with mean 100 and shape 1000, 200, 100, 50, 20 or 10 (coefficient of variation 0.32 to 3.16): positive, skewed right, every moment and $\mathrm E[1/y]$ finite.
D22 [population] log-skewed outcomes: $y = 100\,e^x$ with $x = \pm\sigma(G-\alpha)/\sqrt\alpha$, $G \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha, 1)$: $\ln y$ with standard deviation $\sigma$ (0.5 or 1; sd in the settings' names) and skewness $\pm 2/\sqrt\alpha$ (-2, -1, -0.5, +0.5, +1, +2), and the lognormal of the same $\sigma$ (skewness 0). Some have infinite moments: $\mathrm E[y]$ infinite ($\sigma = 1$, skewness +2), $\mathrm E[y^2]$ infinite ($\sigma = 0.5$, skewness +2; $\sigma = 1$, skewness +1), $\mathrm E[1/y]$ infinite ($\sigma = 1$, skewness -2).
D23 [population] bimodal outcomes: $y = 100\,e^x$, $x$ normal with mean $-1$ (probability $p$) or $+1$, standard deviation 0.25; $p = 0.5$ (equal modes), 0.65 (left mode higher), 0.35 (right mode higher).
D24 [population] outcomes straddling zero: Normal outcomes with standard deviation 1 and mean 0, 0.5 or 1: 50%, 31% and 16% negative; $\mathrm E[1/|y|]$ infinite.
D25 [population] zero-inflated outcomes: Lognormal outcomes (median 100, $\sigma$ 0.5 or 1) replaced by 0 with probability $p_0$: 0.25, 0.42, 0.47, 0.6 for $\sigma = 0.5$ and 0.25, 0.37, 0.42, 0.6 for $\sigma = 1$, on both sides of the zero threshold of C7 (0.4469 and 0.3940).
D26 [population] log-symmetric outcomes: Lognormal outcomes with $\sigma$, and log-Laplace outcomes with scale $b$, each 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 or 6, median 100: $\ln y$ symmetric and strictly unimodal, so that sMAPE and MAE both target the median (C5).
D27 [population] widening outcomes: $\ln y = \ln 100 + \sigma\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ standard normal (lognormal), standard Gumbel (log-Gumbel right, $\ln y$ skewed right) or minus standard Gumbel (log-Gumbel left), for $\sigma$ = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 10.
D30 [population] Poisson counts: Poisson outcomes with means 0.3, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 3 and 10: counts whose share of zeros falls from 0.74 to almost 0.
D35 [population] intermittent demand settings: Demand with many zeros. Counts: Poisson with means 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.5 and 3 (share of zeros from 0.90 to 0.05) and, overdispersed, negative binomial with size 0.5 and means 0.5, 1 and 2 (share of zeros 0.71, 0.58 and 0.45). Non-integer demand: lognormal demand sizes (median 1, $\sigma = 1$) replaced by 0 with probability 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 or 0.8.

C3 "sMAPE is a hyperbolic tangent of the log error" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E20, empirical_quant E31; uses C1; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  For $y > 0$ and $f > 0$, the [[D1:sMAPE term]] is $s = \tanh(|\ln y - \ln f|/2)$ ([[D4:log error]] $\ln y - \ln f$). (a) It is 0 when $y = f$, strictly increasing and concave in $|\ln y - \ln f|$, and bounded by 1, which it approaches as $|\ln y - \ln f|$ grows. (b) $0 \le \tfrac12|\ln y - \ln f| - s \le \tfrac1{24}|\ln y - \ln f|^3$; so when all log errors are small, sMAPE is about 100 times the mean absolute error of $\ln f$ as a forecast of $\ln y$ ([[D19:log-MAE]]). (c) The log-MAE of a test set is minimised by its median; sMAPE is then minimised near the median, not in general at it. For positive actuals $y_1, \dots, y_n$ with a median $m$, a forecast $f^*$ that minimises their sMAPE satisfies
$$\frac1n\sum_t|\ln y_t - \ln f^*| - \frac1n\sum_t|\ln y_t - \ln m| \le \frac1{12}\cdot\frac1n\sum_t|\ln y_t - \ln f^*|^3:$$
its mean absolute log error exceeds the smallest possible by at most a twelfth of its mean cubed absolute log error. The minimiser is the median itself only under further conditions on the distribution of the actuals.
  in plain words: sMAPE measures the error on the log scale. For small errors it is half the absolute log error; for large errors it levels off. For small errors its best forecast is close to the median, but not in general the median.
  extends B12: Tofallis (2015) relates sMAPE to the log accuracy ratio among relative measures; C3 shows the sMAPE term is exactly the hyperbolic tangent of half the absolute log ratio.
  test: formal; supported if The proof of the hypothesis as worded here, in the study's proof documents (proofs.md, proof-suilin.md), is complete, every step naming what it rests on; the independent checker (an agent of another model than the author's: Claude Opus 5.5, registered as an actor before it checks) records a check of the hypothesis as worded with outcome confirmed; and the deciding run's numerical checks of its formulas meet the tolerances of the design.; not supported if A counterexample to the hypothesis as worded, found by the proof, the checker, the supervisor or the deciding run, that a correction of the proof cannot remove.; verdict judged

C4 "sMAPE targets a weighted median" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E22, empirical_quant E31; uses C9; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  Let the actual $y$ be positive and take no single value with positive probability. A forecast $f$ has the lowest expected sMAPE only if the actuals below $f$ and those above it carry equal total [[D5:weight]] $w(y,f) = y/(y+f)^2$:
$$\mathrm E[w(y,f)\,\mathbf 1\{y<f\}] = \mathrm E[w(y,f)\,\mathbf 1\{y>f\}].$$
 So the [[D6:sMAPE target]] is a median of the actuals weighted by $w$, where the ordinary median gives every actual the same weight. An actual's weight is largest when it equals the forecast, is the same for $y/f = r$ and $1/r$, and falls as $y/f$ moves away from 1 in either direction.
  in plain words: For any distribution, the forecast sMAPE rewards balances the actuals below it and above it. Each actual is weighted by how close it is to the forecast by ratio.
  explains B4: C5 found sMAPE's preferred candidate close to the median on other distributions; C4 shows why: the target is a weighted median, on the side of the median that carries more weight (C12).
  addresses B12: Tofallis (2015) states that minimising sMAPE does not produce an established measure of location; C4 identifies the target as a median weighted by y/(y+f)^2.
  qualifies B10: Hewamalage et al. (2023) say sMAPE may tend towards selecting a slightly overestimating model; C4 shows the target is a weighted median, which lies on the side of the median that carries more weight, above or below it (C12).
  qualifies C2: C2 compares two forecasts of one known actual. For an uncertain actual, the forecast sMAPE rewards is the weighted median of C4, not a forecast that is too high.

C6 "Two separate groups: the median is a local maximum" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E24, empirical_quant E1; uses C3, C9, C12; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  Let $\ln y$ have two modes, at $\ln m - a$ and $\ln m + a$ ($a > 0$), with the actuals concentrated near them: a mixture of a distribution near $\ln m - a$ and its mirror image about $\ln m$, with weights $p$ and $1 - p$. (a) With equal weights the median $m$ lies in the gap between the two groups; when the groups are narrow enough it has a higher [[D3:expected sMAPE]] than forecasts near either mode, and it is a local maximum of expected sMAPE; there is a minimiser on each side of the median, and these approach $m\,e^{-a}$ and $m\,e^{a}$ as the groups narrow. (b) With unequal weights and narrow groups, the best forecast is near the mode of the larger group.
  in plain words: When the actuals form two separate groups, the median falls in the gap between them, far from both. So sMAPE scores it worse than forecasts near either group.

C7 "Zero actuals: when the target is zero" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E25, empirical_quant E1; uses C9; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  A forecast of the opposite sign to the actual, a zero forecast of a non-zero actual, and a non-zero forecast of a zero actual all give the [[D1:sMAPE term]] its largest value, 1. Let the actual $y$ be 0 with probability $p_0$ and otherwise positive, and $s_+$ the lowest expected $s$ a positive forecast reaches on the positive actuals. The forecast 0 is the [[D6:sMAPE target]] when $p_0 > (1 - s_+)/(2 - s_+)$, and a positive forecast does when the inequality is reversed; the threshold is below $1/2$ whenever $s_+ > 0$.
  in plain words: The worst possible score goes to forecasts of the wrong sign and to zero forecasts. With enough zeros among the actuals, sMAPE prefers the forecast 0. The share of zeros needed is below one half.
  refines B5: C6 found sMAPE preferring zero on counts with more than half zeros; C7 gives the exact threshold, which is below one half whenever the positive outcomes vary.
  extends B13: Kolassa (2026) shows that sMAPE elicits a zero forecast for sufficiently intermittent actuals; C7 gives the share of zeros at which it does, which is below one half.

C9 "Expected sMAPE is always finite and has a minimiser" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E21, empirical_quant E1; uses C3; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  For actuals and forecasts of any sign, $0 \le s \le 1$. So the [[D3:expected sMAPE]] of every forecast is finite, between 0 and 200, whatever the distribution of the actuals and without any moment of it being finite (unlike RMSE, MAE and MAPE, C8). For a positive random actual $y$, at least one forecast has the lowest expected sMAPE.
  in plain words: The expected sMAPE of a forecast is always finite, even when the actuals have a heavy tail that makes the expected RMSE, MAE or MAPE infinite. And there is always a best forecast.
  extends B10: Hewamalage et al. (2023) note that sMAPE is bounded by 200; C9 adds that its expected value is therefore finite for every distribution and that a best forecast exists.

C10 "Expected sMAPE flattens as the actuals spread out" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E28, empirical_quant E1; uses C3; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  Let $\ln y = \mu + \sigma\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon$ has a fixed density $h$ with bounded continuous first and second derivatives, and write a forecast as $f = e^{\mu + \sigma c}$ (its quantile level, the probability that $\varepsilon \le c$, does not depend on $\sigma$). As $\sigma$ grows, $\mathrm E[s(y,f)] = 1 - 4\ln 2\,h(c)/\sigma + O(\sigma^{-3})$ uniformly in $c$. (a) The [[D3:expected sMAPE]] of every forecast tends to its bound 200, and the difference between forecasts at two fixed quantile levels shrinks like $1/\sigma$. C18 "sMAPE loses its power to tell forecasts apart as the actuals spread out" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E35, empirical_quant E32; uses C3, C10; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  Let $\ln y = \mu + \sigma\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon$ has a fixed density $h$ with bounded continuous first and second derivatives and distribution function $H$, and compare two forecasts at fixed quantile levels, $f_1 = e^{\mu+\sigma c_1}$ and $f_2 = e^{\mu+\sigma c_2}$ with $c_1 < c_2$, by their [[D32:signal-to-noise ratio of a comparison]]. (a) For the [[D1:sMAPE term]], as $\sigma$ grows,
$$\mathrm E[D] = \frac{4\ln 2\,(h(c_1) - h(c_2))}{\sigma} + O(\sigma^{-3}), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(D) = \frac{4(2\ln 2 - 1)(h(c_1)+h(c_2))}{\sigma} + O(\sigma^{-2}).$$
So when $h(c_1) \ne h(c_2)$ the ratio is
$$\frac{2\ln 2}{\sqrt{2\ln 2 - 1}}\,\frac{|h(c_1)-h(c_2)|}{\sqrt{h(c_1)+h(c_2)}}\,\sigma^{-1/2}\,(1 + O(1/\sigma)):$$
it falls to zero like $\sigma^{-1/2}$, and the size of the test set that keeps the mean difference a given number of standard errors from zero grows in proportion to $\sigma$. (b) For the absolute log error $|\ln y - \ln f|$ ([[D19:log-MAE]]), $D = \sigma(|\varepsilon - c_2| - |\varepsilon - c_1|)$, so the ratio, wherever $\mathrm{sd}(D) > 0$, does not depend on $\sigma$. (c) For the absolute error $|y - f|$ ([[D11:MAE]]), as $\sigma$ grows the ratio tends to
$$\frac{|2H(c_2) - 1|}{2\sqrt{H(c_2)(1 - H(c_2))}}$$
when $0 < H(c_2) < 1$; the limit is positive unless the higher forecast is the median. (d) For the squared error $(y-f)^2$ ([[D10:RMSE]]), when $\mathrm E[y^2]$ is finite the ratio is $|2\,\mathrm E[y] - f_1 - f_2|/(2\,\mathrm{sd}(y))$; for lognormal actuals, $e^{\sigma^2/2}$ times the ratio tends to 1 as $\sigma$ grows. It loses it faster, with a ratio of at most order $\sigma^{-5/2}$, when $h(c_1) = h(c_2) > 0$. The squared error on lognormal actuals loses it faster than any power of $\sigma$.

E18 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C1 (section H1 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). Only the ratio of the actual to the forecast matters, not their size.
  S1 Since $y$ and $f$ are positive, $|y| + |f| = y + f$. So $s = |y - f|/(y + f)$. (by D1; algebra)
  S2 Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $f > 0$ gives $\dfrac{|y-f|/f}{(y+f)/f}$. Since $f > 0$, $|y - f|/f = |(y - f)/f| = |r - 1|$. And $(y+f)/f = r + 1$. (by S1; algebra)
  S3 So $s = |r - 1|/(r + 1)$. The contribution depends only on the ratio of actual to forecast. (by S2; conclusion)
E19 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C2 (section H2 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). sMAPE is symmetric in ratios. It favours a high forecast only when errors are compared in absolute terms.
  S1 By C1, $s = |r - 1|/(r + 1)$. Replacing $r$ by $1/r$ gives $\dfrac{|1/r - 1|}{1/r + 1}$. Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by $r > 0$ gives $\dfrac{|1 - r|}{1 + r}$, the same value. (by C1; algebra)
  S2 The forecast $f = ky$ has $r = 1/k$, and $f = y/k$ has $r = k$. By step 1 they score the same. This is (a). (by S1; algebra)
  S3 The forecasts $y + d$ and $y - d$ are both $d$ away from $y$, so both numerators are $d$. The denominators are $y + (y + d) = 2y + d$ and $y + (y - d) = 2y - d > 0$. The larger denominator gives the smaller value. This is (b). (by S2; algebra)
  S4 So sMAPE treats a forecast $k$ times too high and one $k$ times too low alike. It favours the high forecast only when the two are equally wrong in absolute terms. (by S3; conclusion)
E20 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C3 (section H3 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). sMAPE measures the error on the log scale. For small errors it is half the absolute log error; for large errors it levels off.
  S1 Write $z = \ln y - \ln f$, so that $r = y/f = e^z$. By C1, $s = \dfrac{|e^z - 1|}{e^z + 1}$. (by C1; definition)
  S2 Multiplying the numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{e^z - 1}{e^z + 1}$ by $e^{-z/2}$ gives $\dfrac{e^{z/2} - e^{-z/2}}{e^{z/2} + e^{-z/2}} = \tanh(z/2)$. The denominator is positive, so $s = |\tanh(z/2)|$. (by S1; algebra)
  S3 $\tanh$ changes only its sign when its argument changes sign: $\tanh(-x) = -\tanh(x)$, as the definition shows. So $|\tanh(z/2)| = \tanh(|z|/2)$, which is the stated form. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 For (a): $\tanh$ is 0 at 0. On $[0, \infty)$ it is strictly increasing and concave, and it approaches 1 as its argument grows; its derivative $1 - \tanh^2 x$ is positive and decreasing for $x > 0$. With the argument $|\ln y - \ln f|/2$, $s$ has these properties in $|\ln y - \ln f|$. (by S3; monotonicity)
  S5 For (b): for every $x \ge 0$, $x - x^3/3 \le \tanh x \le x$. The upper bound holds because $\tanh$ has slope at most 1; the lower one because the derivative of $\tanh x - x + x^3/3$ is $x^2 - \tanh^2 x \ge 0$, and the difference is 0 at $x = 0$. Putting $x = |\ln y - \ln f|/2$ gives the bounds. (by S4; algebra)
  S6 For (c), the median: the mean of $|\ln y_t - \ln f|$ over a test set is minimised when $\ln f$ is a median of the $\ln y_t$. Since the logarithm is increasing, this is when $f$ is a median $m$ of the $y_t$. (by S5; monotonicity)
  S7 For (c), the bound: with $z_t = \ln y_t - \ln f$ the log errors of the test set, write $\overline{|z|}(f) = \frac1n\sum_t|\ln y_t - \ln f|$, $\overline{|z|^3}(f) = \frac1n\sum_t|\ln y_t - \ln f|^3$ and $\bar s(f)$ for the mean of the sMAPE terms of the test set. Averaging the bounds of step 5 over the test set gives $\bar s(f) \le \tfrac12\overline{|z|}(f) \le \bar s(f) + \tfrac1{24}\overline{|z|^3}(f)$ for every $f > 0$. Let $f^*$ minimise $\bar s$, so that $\bar s(f^*) \le \bar s(m)$. Then
$$\tfrac12\overline{|z|}(f^*) \le \bar s(f^*) + \tfrac1{24}\overline{|z|^3}(f^*) \le \bar s(m) + \tfrac1{24}\overline{|z|^3}(f^*) \le \tfrac12\overline{|z|}(m) + \tfrac1{24}\overline{|z|^3}(f^*).$$
Multiplying by 2 gives $\overline{|z|}(f^*) - \overline{|z|}(m) \le \tfrac1{12}\overline{|z|^3}(f^*)$, the stated inequality. (by S5; algebra)
  S8 For (c), not the median in general: take the five actuals $1, 1, e, e^4, e^4$, whose median is $e$. With the forecast at $e$ the log errors are $1, 1, 0, 3, 3$ in size and the sMAPE terms sum to $2\tanh(0.5) + 2\tanh(1.5) = 2.734$. With the forecast at 1 the log errors are $0, 0, 1, 4, 4$ and the terms sum to $\tanh(0.5) + 2\tanh(2) = 2.390$, which is lower. So the median does not minimise the sMAPE of this test set, although it minimises its log-MAE (step 6). (by S6; algebra)
  S9 So $s = \tanh(|\ln y - \ln f|/2)$. It rises from 0 like half the absolute log error and levels off at 1. For small errors sMAPE is close to the log-MAE, and its minimiser is close to the median without in general being the median. (by S8; conclusion)
E21 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C9 (section H4 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). The expected values of RMSE, MAE and MAPE become infinite for every forecast when the actuals have an infinite moment, such as an infinite mean (a heavy tail, C8). The expected sMAPE never does, and there is always a best forecast.
  S1 Finite. For positive values, $s = \tanh(|\ln y - \ln f|/2)$ (C3), which lies between 0 and 1. For any signs, $|y - f| \le |y| + |f|$, so again $s \le 1$. A quantity between 0 and 1 has an expectation between 0 and 1, so the expected sMAPE is between 0 and 200, whatever the actuals. (by C3; earlier result)
  S2 A best forecast. Write $\mathrm E[s(y,f)]$ for the expectation over the random actual $y$. As $f$ approaches 0 or grows without bound, $|\ln y - \ln f|$ grows for every positive value of $y$, so $s$ approaches 1 (C3), and so does its expectation. For every positive $f$ it is below 1. It changes continuously with $f$, because $s$ does and is at most 1 (bounded convergence). A continuous function that is below 1 in between and returns towards 1 at both ends reaches a lowest value at some $f > 0$. A forecast of 0 or a negative forecast scores 1 on every positive actual, so it is never better. (by C3, D1; bounded convergence)
  S3 So the expected sMAPE is always finite, whatever the tails of the actuals. For positive actuals, a forecast with the lowest expected sMAPE always exists. (by S2; conclusion)
E22 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C4 (section H5 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). For any distribution, the best forecast balances the actuals below it and above it. Each actual is weighted by how close it is to the forecast by ratio.
  S1 The derivative of $s$. When $y > f > 0$, $s = (y - f)/(y + f)$. By the quotient rule its derivative in $f$ is $\dfrac{-(y+f) - (y-f)}{(y+f)^2} = -2w(y,f)$. When $f > y > 0$, $s = (f - y)/(y + f)$, and in the same way its derivative is $2w(y,f)$. Together: for $y \ne f$, the derivative is $2w(y,f)(\mathbf 1\{y < f\} - \mathbf 1\{y > f\})$. (by D1; quotient rule)
  S2 A bound on it. Since $(y+f)^2 \ge 4yf$, $w(y,f) \le 1/(4f)$. So the derivative of $s$ is at most $1/(2f)$ in size, whatever $y$, and at most $1/f$ for every forecast above $f/2$. The only actual at which $s$ has no derivative is $y = f$, which has probability zero. (by S1; algebra)
  S3 The derivative of the expectation. With this bound, the derivative may be taken inside the expectation (dominated convergence):
$$\frac{d}{df}\,\mathrm E[s(y,f)] = 2\,\mathrm E\big[w(y,f)\,(\mathbf 1\{y < f\} - \mathbf 1\{y > f\})\big].$$ (by S2; dominated convergence)
  S4 The balance. A forecast with the lowest expected sMAPE exists (C9). There this derivative is 0, which is the stated balance: half the total weight lies below $f$ and half above. (by C9; earlier result)
  S5 The weight. With $r = y/f$, $w(y,f) = \dfrac1f\cdot\dfrac{r}{(1+r)^2} = \dfrac1f\cdot\dfrac{1}{r + 2 + 1/r}$. The sum $r + 1/r$ is the same for $r$ and $1/r$. It is smallest, 2, at $r = 1$, and grows as $r$ moves away from 1. So $w$ is largest at $y = f$ and falls as $y/f$ moves away from 1, equally for $r$ and $1/r$. (by S4; algebra)
  S6 So sMAPE targets a weighted median of the actuals. Each actual is weighted by how close it is to the forecast by ratio. (by S5; conclusion)
E23 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C5 (section H6 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). This is the case of C4 in which the weights balance at the median.
  S1 By C3, $\mathrm E[s(y,f)] = \mathrm E\big[\tanh\big(|\ln y - \ln f|/2\big)\big]$. Take a forecast $f = m\,e^{\tau}$ with $\tau > 0$; the case $\tau < 0$ is the mirror image. We show that its expected sMAPE is larger than that of $m$. (by C3; setup)
  S2 Write both expectations as integrals over $v$, the distance of $\ln y$ from $\ln f$. For the forecast $m\,e^{\tau}$, $\ln y = \ln m + \tau + v$; for $m$, $\ln y = \ln m + v$. Their difference is
$$\Delta = \int \tanh(|v|/2)\,\big[g(\ln m+\tau+v) - g(\ln m+v)\big]\,dv.$$ (by S1; definition)
  S3 Write $\phi(v) = g(\ln m+\tau+v) - g(\ln m+v)$. By the symmetry of $g$ about $\ln m$, $\phi(-\tau-v) = g(\ln m-v) - g(\ln m-\tau-v) = g(\ln m+v) - g(\ln m+\tau+v) = -\phi(v)$. So reflecting $v$ about $-\tau/2$ reverses the sign of $\phi$. (by S2; definition)
  S4 Split the integral at $-\tau/2$ and reflect its left part onto the right. This gives
$$\Delta = \int_{v > -\tau/2} \big[\tanh(|v|/2) - \tanh(|v+\tau|/2)\big]\,\phi(v)\,dv.$$ (by S3; algebra)
  S5 For $v > -\tau/2$, $v$ is closer to 0 than $v + \tau$ is: $|v| < |v + \tau|$. So the bracket is negative, because $\tanh$ is increasing (C3). And $\phi(v) \le 0$ by unimodality: $g(\ln m+\tau+v) \le g(\ln m+v)$. Their product is $\ge 0$, and $> 0$ on a set of positive probability. So $\Delta > 0$. (by C3; monotonicity)
  S6 So every forecast other than the median has a larger expected sMAPE. The median is the only forecast that minimises it. (by S5; conclusion)
E24 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C6 (section H7 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). When the actuals form two separate groups, the median falls in the gap between them, far from both. So sMAPE scores it worse than forecasts near either group.
  S1 Write forecasts on the log scale as $\ln f = \ln m + c$. By C3, the expected contribution is $\mathrm E[\tanh(|\ln y - \ln m - c|/2)]$, a function of $c$. (by C3; setup)
  S2 Why the median is bad. Take the groups to be single points first. A forecast at the median is $a$ away, on the log scale, from every actual. So its expected $s$ is $\tanh(a/2)$. (by S1; case analysis)
  S3 A forecast at the mode, $\ln f = \ln m + a$, is exact for half the actuals and $2a$ away from the other half. So its expected $s$ is $\tfrac12\tanh(a)$. Since $\tanh(a) = \dfrac{2\tanh(a/2)}{1 + \tanh^2(a/2)}$, this is $\dfrac{\tanh(a/2)}{1 + \tanh^2(a/2)} < \tanh(a/2)$. The mode is better. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 The expected $s$ changes continuously as the groups widen from points, because $s$ is bounded and continuous. So the mode is still better for narrow groups. (by S3; continuity)
  S5 Why the median is a local maximum. By C12, step 4, the second derivative of the expected contribution at the median is
$$g(\ln m) - \tfrac12\,\mathrm E\Big[\operatorname{sech}^2\Big(\frac{\ln y - \ln m}{2}\Big)\tanh\Big(\frac{|\ln y - \ln m|}{2}\Big)\Big].$$ (by C12; earlier result)
  S6 By symmetry the first derivative at the median is 0. When the groups narrow, few actuals lie near the median, so $g(\ln m)$ tends to 0. Meanwhile the expectation tends to $\operatorname{sech}^2(a/2)\tanh(a/2) > 0$. So the second derivative becomes negative, and the median is a local maximum. (by S5; symmetry)
  S7 A minimiser on each side. The expected sMAPE has a lowest point (C9). It is not at the local maximum $c = 0$. By symmetry, if $c^*$ is a minimiser, so is $-c^*$. (by C9; symmetry)
  S8 Near the modes. Write $\tanh(|z|/2) = 1 - 2\lambda(z)$, with $\lambda(z) = 1/(1 + e^{|z|})$. The function $\lambda$ is largest at 0 and falls as $|z|$ grows, more slowly the larger $|z|$ is. Minimising the expected $s$ is maximising $\mathrm E[\lambda(\ln y - \ln m - c)]$. As the groups narrow, this approaches $p\,\lambda(c + a) + (1 - p)\,\lambda(c - a)$, uniformly in $c$. (by S7; definition)
  S9 With equal weights, take $0 < c < a$. The derivative of this limit is half the rate at which $\lambda$ falls at distance $a - c$, minus half the rate at distance $a + c$. It is positive, because $\lambda$ falls faster nearer 0. For $c > a$ the limit decreases. So on $c > 0$ its only maximum is at $c = a$, and the minimisers on that side approach $a$. By symmetry, those on the other side approach $-a$. This proves (a). (by S8; symmetry)
  S10 Unequal weights. The limit increases up to $c = -a$ and decreases after $c = a$. In between it is convex, by the argument of step 9. So its maximum is at $-a$ or at $a$. The difference between these two values is $(2p - 1)\big(\lambda(0) - \lambda(2a)\big)$, which is positive when $p > 1/2$. So the maximum, and with it the best forecast for narrow groups, is at the mode of the larger group. This proves (b). (by S9; convexity)
  S11 So with two separate groups of actuals, the median is a hump of the expected sMAPE. The best forecasts are near the modes, and with unequal groups the larger one wins. As the weights pass through equality, the target jumps from one mode to the other. (by S10; conclusion)
E25 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C7 (section H8 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). The worst possible score goes to forecasts of the wrong sign and to zero forecasts. With enough zeros among the actuals, sMAPE prefers the forecast 0. The share of zeros needed is below one half.
  S1 When $y$ and $f$ have opposite signs, $|y - f| = |y| + |f|$, so $s = 1$. When exactly one of them is 0, the numerator and the denominator both equal the absolute value of the other one, so $s = 1$. (by D1; algebra)
  S2 A positive forecast scores 1 on every zero actual (step 1). So its expected $s$ is $p_0$ plus $(1 - p_0)$ times its expected $s$ on the positive actuals. This is at least $p_0 + (1-p_0)\,s_+$, with equality at the best positive forecasts, which exist (C9). (by S1, C9; earlier result)
  S3 The forecast 0 scores 0 on zero actuals and 1 on positive ones. So its expected $s$ is $1 - p_0$. A negative forecast scores 1 on every actual, which is never better than 0. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 The forecast 0 is better when $1 - p_0 < p_0 + (1 - p_0)s_+$. Collecting the terms in $p_0$ gives $1 - s_+ < p_0(2 - s_+)$. Since $2 - s_+ > 0$, this is the stated inequality. (by S3; algebra)
  S5 Compare the threshold with $1/2$: $\dfrac{1 - s_+}{2 - s_+} < \dfrac12$ exactly when $2 - 2s_+ < 2 - s_+$, that is, when $s_+ > 0$. And $s_+ > 0$ unless the positive actuals are all one value. (by S4; algebra)
  S6 So sMAPE switches to the forecast 0 once zeros are frequent enough, and the share needed is below one half. At the threshold, the target jumps from a positive forecast to 0. (by S5; conclusion)
E26 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C11 (section H9 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). When actuals can be negative or positive, sMAPE's target is never near zero. It is on the same side of zero as the mean. So a small shift of the mean across zero makes the target jump from a negative value to a positive one.
  S1 A positive forecast against its mirror image. Take $f > 0$ and compare it with $-f$. An actual $y > 0$ has the sign of $f$, so it scores less than 1 with $f$, and exactly 1 with $-f$ (C7). (by C7; symmetry)
  S2 The mirror actual $-y$ scores 1 with $f$. By symmetry, it scores the same amount less than 1 with $-f$. So each pair of actuals $y$ and $-y$ favours $f$ when $y$ is the more likely of the two, and $-f$ otherwise. (by S1; symmetry)
  S3 When $\mu > 0$, every positive actual $y$ is more likely than $-y$, because $y$ is closer to $\mu$ and the density of the actuals falls away from $\mu$. So every pair favours $f$, and $f$ has the lower expected sMAPE. Every best forecast is therefore positive. The case $\mu < 0$ is the mirror image. (by S2; symmetry)
  S4 Best forecasts exist on each side. For a forecast $f > 0$, the actuals of its sign score below 1 and have positive probability, so its expected $s$ is below 1. As $f$ approaches 0 or grows without bound, every non-zero actual scores close to 1, so the expected $s$ approaches 1. As in C9, the expected $s$ is continuous in $f$ and so reaches a lowest value for $f > 0$; the same holds for $f < 0$. (by C9; continuity)
  S5 Away from zero, uniformly. Fix a forecast $f_1 > 0$. For every $\mu$ in a range around 0, say $|\mu| \le 1$, its expected $s$ is at most some value $q < 1$, since it changes continuously with $\mu$. For a forecast $f$ with $|f| \le \eta$, every actual with $|y| \ge \sqrt\eta$ scores at least $(\sqrt\eta - \eta)/(\sqrt\eta + \eta)$, which approaches 1 as $\eta$ shrinks; and the share of actuals with $|y| < \sqrt\eta$ is small for every such $\mu$: since $\varepsilon$ is symmetric and its density decreases away from 0, an interval of a given length around 0 holds at least as much of its probability as any other interval of that length, so $P(|\mu + \varepsilon| < \sqrt\eta) \le P(|\varepsilon| < \sqrt\eta)$, which tends to 0 as $\eta$ shrinks. So for $\eta$ small enough, every forecast within $\eta$ of 0 has an expected $s$ above $q$, for every $\mu$ in the range: it is worse than $f_1$ (or its mirror image). The best forecasts therefore stay at least $\eta$ from 0. (by S4; symmetry)
  S6 So the target is always on the same side of zero as the mean, and never near zero. It jumps from negative to positive as the mean crosses zero. (by S5; conclusion)
E27 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C8 (section H10 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). Each of the other three measures has an infinite expected value, for every forecast, when a moment it needs is infinite. What it then rewards depends on the measure.
  S1 (a) RMSE. If $\mathrm E[y^2]$ is finite, so is $\mathrm E[y]$. Expanding the square gives $\mathrm E[(y-f)^2] = \mathrm E[y^2] - 2f\,\mathrm E[y] + f^2$, which is finite. Conversely, $y^2 \le 2(y - f)^2 + 2f^2$. So a finite $\mathrm E[(y-f)^2]$ makes $\mathrm E[y^2]$ finite. (by D10; algebra)
  S2 Expanding both squares gives $(y - f_1)^2 - (y - f_2)^2 = (f_2 - f_1)(2y - f_1 - f_2)$. Its expectation is finite when $\mathrm E[y]$ is, and equals $(f_1 - f_2)(f_1 + f_2 - 2\,\mathrm E[y]) = (f_1 - \mathrm E[y])^2 - (f_2 - \mathrm E[y])^2$. (by S1; algebra)
  S3 For $f_1 = \mathrm E[y]$ this is negative for every $f_2 \ne \mathrm E[y]$. So the mean is preferred. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 If $\mathrm E[y]$ is infinite, then for $f_2 > f_1$ the expectation in step 2 is $+\infty$. Every forecast is beaten by any larger one, so none is preferred to all others. (by S2; algebra)
  S5 (b) MAE. Since $|y - f| \ge y - f$, $\mathrm E|y - f|$ is infinite when $\mathrm E[y]$ is. Otherwise it is at most $\mathrm E[y] + |f|$. (by D11; algebra)
  S6 The difference $|y - f_1| - |y - f_2|$ is at most $|f_1 - f_2|$ in size, so its expectation is finite. (by S5; algebra)
  S7 The derivative of $|y - f|$ in $f$ is $\mathbf 1\{y < f\} - \mathbf 1\{y > f\}$. The expectation of this is $P(y < f) - P(y > f)$, which is negative below a median and positive above it. So a median is preferred. (by S6; algebra)
  S8 (c) MAPE. For $f > 0$, the contribution of an actual to MAPE, divided by 100, is $|y - f|/y = |1 - f/y|$. This is at least $f/y - 1$. So the expected MAPE is infinite when $\mathrm E[1/y]$ is, and at most $1 + f\,\mathrm E[1/y]$ otherwise. (by D12; algebra)
  S9 As a function of $f$, $|1 - f/y|$ is convex. Its derivative is $(1/y)(\mathbf 1\{y < f\} - \mathbf 1\{y > f\})$. Its expectation is 0 where the actuals below $f$ and those above it have equal total weight $1/y$: at a median of the actuals weighted by $1/y$. (by S8; convexity)
  S10 The forecast 0 gives $|1 - 0| = 1$ for every actual, so MAPE 100. A negative forecast gives more than 1. So when $\mathrm E[1/y]$ is infinite, 0 is the only forecast with a finite expected MAPE. (by S9; algebra)
  S11 If $y = 0$ with positive probability: for $f \ne 0$ that actual contributes $|f|/0$, which is infinite. For $f = 0$ it contributes $0/0$, which is undefined. (by S10; algebra)
  S12 So each of RMSE, MAE and MAPE has an infinite expected value when a moment it needs is infinite. RMSE and MAE still rank forecasts by their differences. MAPE turns to the forecast 0, and with zero actuals it targets nothing. (by S11; conclusion)
E28 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C10 (section H11 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). For widely spread actuals, every forecast scores close to the worst value. The differences between forecasts shrink, and the target moves from the median to the mode of $\ln y$.
  S1 Rewrite the expectation. By C3, and since $\tanh(|x|/2) = 1 - \dfrac{2}{1 + e^{|x|}}$, $\mathrm E[s(y,f)] = 1 - \mathrm E\big[2/(1 + e^{|\ln y - \ln f|})\big]$. The density of $\ln y$ at $x$ is $h\big((x - \mu)/\sigma\big)/\sigma$. Substituting $x = \ln f + v$ in the integral gives
$$\mathrm E[s(y,f)] = 1 - \frac1\sigma\int \frac{2}{1 + e^{|v|}}\,h\Big(c + \frac v\sigma\Big)\,dv.$$ (by C3; change of variables)
  S2 Expand $h$. By Taylor's theorem, $h(c + v/\sigma) = h(c) + h'(c)\,v/\sigma + \tfrac12 h''(\xi)\,v^2/\sigma^2$ for some $\xi$ between $c$ and $c + v/\sigma$. (by S1; Taylor's theorem)
  S3 The first term integrates to $4\ln 2\,h(c)$, since $\int \frac{2}{1+e^{|v|}}\,dv = 4\ln 2$. Over $v > 0$, $\int 2/(1 + e^v)\,dv = 2\ln 2$. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 The second term integrates to 0: its factor $v$ changes sign with $v$, while the rest does not. (by S3; algebra)
  S5 The third term is at most $\tfrac12\max|h''|$ times $\int \frac{2v^2}{1+e^{|v|}}\,dv = 6\zeta(3) \approx 7.21$, divided by $\sigma^2$. This integral is a standard Fermi–Dirac integral. With the factor $1/\sigma$ of step 1, this gives the expansion. (by S1; standard integral)
  S6 (a). The expansion tends to 1, so the expected sMAPE tends to 200. Two forecasts at fixed quantile levels have fixed positions $c_1$ and $c_2$. So their difference is $4\ln 2\,[h(c_2) - h(c_1)]/\sigma + O(\sigma^{-3})$. (by S5; algebra)
  S7 (b). Minimising the expansion over $c$ is maximising $h(c)$, up to a term that shrinks like $\sigma^{-2}$ for every $c$ at once. A density with a bounded derivative tends to 0 far out. So its single highest point $c_0$ stands clear of every other point, and the minimisers' positions tend to $c_0$. So do their quantile levels. (by S6; algebra)
  S8 (c). By C3 (b), $\tfrac12\sigma|\varepsilon - c| - s$ lies between 0 and $\tfrac1{24}\sigma^3|\varepsilon - c|^3$. So for $c$ in any bounded range, $\mathrm E[s]/\sigma = \tfrac12\mathrm E|\varepsilon - c| + O(\sigma^2)$. This uses $\mathrm E|\varepsilon|^3 < \infty$. And $\mathrm E|\varepsilon - c|$ is smallest at the median of $\varepsilon$. (by C3; earlier result)
  S9 The minimisers also stay in a bounded range. For large $|c|$, most actuals have $\varepsilon$ at least $|c|/2$ from $c$. And $\tanh x \ge x\tanh 1$ for $0 \le x \le 1$. So $\mathrm E[s]/\sigma$ grows with $|c|$, or exceeds a multiple of $1/\sigma$, beyond its value at the median. So the minimisers' positions tend to the median of $\varepsilon$, whose quantile level is $1/2$. (by S8; algebra)
  S10 So as the actuals spread out, the expected sMAPE flattens towards its bound. Differences between forecasts vanish like $1/\sigma$. The target moves from the median to the mode of $\ln y$. (by S9; conclusion)
E29 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C12 (section H12 of the proof document A1, rendered in A2). The target is above the ordinary median when the actuals above it carry more weight, and below it when those below carry more. When the weights balance and enough actuals lie near the median, the median is the target. This is so when the log of the actual is symmetric with a single peak.
  S1 The derivative at the median. By C4, step 3, the derivative of the expected sMAPE term in $f$ is $2\,\mathrm E[w(y,f)(\mathbf 1\{y < f\} - \mathbf 1\{y > f\})]$. At $f = m$ it is twice the total weight of the actuals below $m$ minus that of the actuals above $m$. (by C4; earlier result)
  S2 The shape of expected sMAPE. With a single local minimum, the expected sMAPE decreases up to it and increases after it. Otherwise it would turn again, at a local maximum. After that turn it would have to fall and then rise once more, because it returns towards 1 at both ends (C9). That would give a second local minimum. (by C9; earlier result)
  S3 (a). If the actuals above $m$ carry more weight, the derivative at the median is negative (step 1): the expected sMAPE is decreasing at the median. By step 2, the median then lies before the minimum, so the target is above the median. The case where the actuals below carry more weight is the mirror image. (by S1, S2; symmetry)
  S4 The second derivative. Measure the forecast on the log scale, $c = \ln f$, so that the expected contribution is $\mathrm E[\tanh(|\ln y - c|/2)]$ (C3). The derivative of $\tanh(|x - c|/2)$ in $c$ is $-\tfrac12\operatorname{sech}^2\big(\tfrac{x-c}{2}\big)$ for $c < x$ and $+\tfrac12\operatorname{sech}^2\big(\tfrac{x-c}{2}\big)$ for $c > x$: it jumps by 1 at $c = x$. Differentiating again and taking the expectation over $x = \ln y$, the jumps add the density $g$ of $\ln y$ at $c$, and elsewhere the second derivative is $-\tfrac12\operatorname{sech}^2\big(\tfrac{x-c}{2}\big)\tanh\big(\tfrac{|x-c|}{2}\big)$. So the second derivative of the expected contribution is
$$g(c) - \tfrac12\,\mathrm E\Big[\operatorname{sech}^2\Big(\frac{\ln y - c}{2}\Big)\tanh\Big(\frac{|\ln y - c|}{2}\Big)\Big].$$
This holds when $g$ is continuous at $c$: since $\operatorname{sech}^2 = 1 - \tanh^2$, the first derivative is $\mathrm P(\ln y \le c) - \tfrac12$, whose derivative at $c$ is $g(c)$, plus the expectation of $-\tfrac12\tanh^2\big(\tfrac{x-c}{2}\big)$ with the sign of $c - x$, a function of $c$ with a bounded continuous derivative, which may be differentiated inside the expectation. (by C3; continuity)
  S5 (b), the sufficient condition. If the two weights are equal, the first derivative at the median is 0 (step 1): the expected sMAPE is flat there. If, in addition, the stated inequality holds, the second derivative at $c = \ln m$ is positive (step 4), so the median is a strict local minimum. With a single local minimum, it is the target. (by S1, S4; algebra)
  S6 (b), without the inequality. A point where the first derivative is 0 can be a flat point of inflection, through which the expected sMAPE keeps rising (or keeps falling) without turning. An example, built by the independent checker of this section: let $\ln y - \ln m$ be a mixture of three normal distributions, with mean $-1.5$ and standard deviation $0.3$ (weight 0.406125), mean 0 and standard deviation $0.5$ (weight 0.054825), and mean $1.158664$ and standard deviation 1 (weight 0.539050); these values are rounded from the exact solution of the three conditions. Its median is $m$, the two weights are equal there, and the second derivative of step 4 is 0 at $\ln m$; the expected sMAPE rises through $m$, where it is flat, and its only minimum is near $\ln f = \ln m - 1.25$. So equal weights alone do not make the median the target. (by S4; algebra)
  S7 (c). By C5 the median is then the only forecast with the lowest expected sMAPE (a density cannot be constant, so a symmetric unimodal density is strictly lower somewhere away from its centre, as C5 asks). For lognormal actuals $\ln y$ has a normal density, and for log-Laplace actuals the Laplace density $e^{-|x - \ln m|/b}/(2b)$; both are symmetric about $\ln m$ and strictly decreasing in the distance from it. (by C5; earlier result)
  S8 So the target is above the ordinary median when the actuals above it carry more of the weight, and below it when those below carry more. When the two balance, the median is the target if, in addition, enough actuals lie near it, as the stated inequality says; this is the case when the log of the actual is symmetric with a single peak. (by S7; conclusion)

  S7 (b). For the absolute log error, $|\ln y - \ln f_i| = \sigma|\varepsilon - c_i|$, so $D = \sigma(|\varepsilon - c_2| - |\varepsilon - c_1|)$. Both are $\sigma$ times those of $|\varepsilon - c_2| - |\varepsilon - c_1|$, whose distribution does not depend on $\sigma$. So their ratio, wherever the standard deviation is not zero, does not depend on $\sigma$. (by S6; algebra)

  S8 The variance. $\mathrm E[\psi(x)^2] = \tfrac14\mathrm E[\operatorname{sech}^4(x/2)] = B/4$, and the square of the second derivative is $\tfrac14(2g(\ln m) - A)^2$ (step 6). So the variance is $B/(2g(\ln m) - A)^2$. (by S6; algebra)
  S9 The sample median. The logarithm of the sample median of the $y_t$ is the sample median of the $\ln y_t$, which minimises the mean of $|x_t - \theta|$. The same theorem applies with $|x - \theta|$ in place of $\tanh(|x - \theta|/2)$: the derivative at 0 is $-1$ for $x > 0$ and $+1$ for $x < 0$, with square 1; the criterion changes by at most $|\theta_1 - \theta_2|$; and the second derivative of $\mathrm E|x - \theta|$ at 0 is $2g(\ln m)$, positive because $g$ is largest at $\ln m$. So the variance is $1/(4g(\ln m)^2)$. (by S8, B15; cited theorem)
  S10 (b), the ratio. Dividing the variance of step 9 by that of step 8 gives $\mathrm{ARE} = (2g(\ln m) - A)^2/(4g(\ln m)^2 B)$. (by S9, S8; algebra)
  S11 (c), narrow distributions. Write $\ln y = \ln m + \sigma\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ standard normal, or Laplace with scale 1 and $\sigma = b$; then $g(\ln m) = h(0)/\sigma$ with $h$ the density of $\varepsilon$, and $x = \sigma\varepsilon$. As $\sigma \to 0$, $A \to 0$ and $B \to 1$ by bounded convergence, and $A/(2g(\ln m)) = \sigma A/(2h(0)) \to 0$. So $\mathrm{ARE} = (1 - A/(2g(\ln m)))^2/B \to 1$. (by S10; bounded convergence)
  S12 (c), wide lognormal distributions. Since $\int\kappa = 2$ (step 2), $2g(\ln m) - A = \int\kappa(u)\,[g(\ln m) - g(\ln m + u)]\,du$. For the normal density, $g(\ln m) - g(\ln m + u) = \big(1 - e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}\big)/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$, and $\tfrac{u^2}{2\sigma^2} - \tfrac{u^4}{8\sigma^4} \le 1 - e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)} \le \tfrac{u^2}{2\sigma^2}$. Integrating by parts, $\int u^2\kappa(u)\,du = 4\int_0^\infty u\operatorname{sech}^2(u/2)\,du = 16\ln 2$; and $\int u^4\kappa(u)\,du$ is finite. So $2g(\ln m) - A = 8\ln 2/(\sigma^3\sqrt{2\pi}) + O(\sigma^{-5})$. In the same way, with $\int\operatorname{sech}^4(u/2)\,du = 8/3$, $B = 8/(3\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}) + O(\sigma^{-3})$. With $g(\ln m) = 1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$, $\mathrm{ARE}\,\sigma^3$ tends to $\dfrac{64(\ln 2)^2}{2\pi}\cdot\dfrac{2\pi}{4}\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt{2\pi}}{8} = 6(\ln 2)^2\sqrt{2\pi}$. (by S2; algebra)
  S13 (c), wide log-Laplace distributions. Here $g(\ln m + u) = e^{-|u|/b}/(2b)$, so $g(\ln m) - g(\ln m + u) = (1 - e^{-|u|/b})/(2b)$, which lies between $\big(|u|/b - u^2/(2b^2)\big)/(2b)$ and $|u|/(2b^2)$. Integrating by parts, $\int |u|\,\kappa(u)\,du = 2\int_0^\infty\operatorname{sech}^2(u/2)\,du = 4$. So $2g(\ln m) - A = 2/b^2 + O(b^{-3})$, and in the same way $B = 4/(3b) + O(b^{-2})$. With $g(\ln m) = 1/(2b)$, $\mathrm{ARE}\,b$ tends to $\dfrac{4}{b^4}\cdot b^2\cdot\dfrac{3b}{4}\cdot b = 3$. (by S12; algebra)
  S14 So the sMAPE minimiser of a test set is asymptotically normal about the median, with a variance that the density at the median and two expectations give. On lognormal and log-Laplace actuals it is asymptotically as precise as the sample median for narrow distributions and asymptotically loses all of its precision relative to it as the actuals spread out, at the rates $\sigma^{-3}$ and $b^{-1}$. (by S13; conclusion)
E30 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C17 (the first proof of the proof document A5). The Suilin loss behaves like sMAPE except near zero, where it becomes an absolute error.
  S1 Which branch of the maximum. $\ell + \delta \ge 0.5 + \delta$ exactly when $\ell \ge 0.5$. So the denominator of $\tilde s$ is $\ell + \delta$ when $\ell \ge 0.5$, and $0.5 + \delta$ when $\ell < 0.5$. (by D28; algebra)
  S2 (a), the identity. When $\ell \ge 0.5$, $\ell > 0$, so $s = |y-f|/\ell$ and $|y-f| = s\,\ell$. By step 1, $\tilde s = |y-f|/(\ell+\delta) = s\,\ell/(\ell+\delta)$. (by S1; algebra)
  S3 (a), the factor. The function $\ell \mapsto \ell/(\ell+\delta) = 1 - \delta/(\ell+\delta)$ is increasing in $\ell$, below 1, and tends to 1 as $\ell$ grows. At $\ell = 0.5$ it equals $0.5/0.6 = 5/6$. So for $\ell \ge 0.5$ it lies in $[5/6, 1)$. (by S2; monotonicity)
  S4 (a), close to $s$, but not a function of the ratio. By steps 2 and 3, $0 \le s - \tilde s = s\,\delta/(\ell+\delta) \le \delta/(\ell+\delta)$, since $s \le 1$ (step 7). So $\tilde s$ is within $\delta/(\ell+\delta)$ of $s$, which shrinks as $\ell$ grows. But $\tilde s$ depends on the scale: $(y,f) = (1,2)$ and $(10,20)$ have the same ratio and the same $s = 1/3$, while $\tilde s = 1/3.1$ and $10/30.1$. So $\tilde s$ is not a function of $y/f$ alone. (by S2, S3; algebra)
  S5 (a), counts. If $y \ge 1$, then $\ell \ge 1 \ge 0.5$. If $y = 0$, then $\ell = |f|$, which is at least 0.5 unless $|f| < 0.5$. So on counts only a zero actual with $|f| < 0.5$ has $\ell < 0.5$. (by S4; algebra)
  S6 (b). When $\ell < 0.5$, by step 1, $\tilde s = |y-f|/(0.5+\delta)$. For $y = f = 0$ this is 0. For $y = 0$ and $|f| < 0.5$ it is $|f|/(0.5+\delta)$. And $s(0,f) = |f|/|f| = 1$ for $f \ne 0$, the largest value of $s$ (step 7). (by S1; algebra)
  S7 (c). $|y - f| \le |y| + |f| = \ell$ (the triangle inequality), so $s \le 1$. When $\ell \ge 0.5$, $\tilde s \le \ell/(\ell+\delta) < 1$ (step 3). When $\ell < 0.5$, $\tilde s \le \ell/(0.5+\delta) < 0.5/0.6 < 1$. And $\tilde s \ge 0$ because it is a ratio of a non-negative number to a positive one. A quantity between 0 and 1 has an expectation between 0 and 1, whatever the joint distribution of actuals and forecasts, so the expected Suilin loss is between 0 and 200. (by S3; algebra)
  S8 So the Suilin term is the sMAPE term shrunk by a factor between 5/6 and 1 wherever $|y| + |f| \ge 0.5$, and an absolute error where $|y| + |f| < 0.5$: it differs from sMAPE in kind only near zero. (by S7; conclusion)
E37 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and real analysis] Proof of C20 (the second proof of the proof document A5). The msMAPE term is the sMAPE term except for actuals below its constant, where a zero actual no longer gives every non-zero forecast the largest value.
  S1 (a). When $y \ge \nu$, $\max\{y,\nu\} = y$, and $y > 0$ because $\nu > 0$. So $s_\nu = |f-y|/(f+y)$, which is $s(y,f)$ for non-negative $y$ and $f$. If every actual is at least $\nu$, the two measures are then the same function of the forecast on every test set and under every distribution of such actuals, so they have the same minimisers. (by D33; algebra)
  S2 (b). When $y < \nu$, $\max\{y,\nu\} = \nu$, so $s_\nu = |f-y|/(f+\nu)$. For $y = 0$ this is $f/(f+\nu) = 1 - \nu/(f+\nu)$, which is 0 at $f = 0$, increasing in $f$, below 1, and tends to 1 as $f$ grows. The sMAPE term of a zero actual is $|f|/|f| = 1$ for every $f > 0$. (by S1; monotonicity)
  S3 (c), the bounds. $s_\nu \ge 0$ because its numerator is non-negative and its denominator is at least $\nu > 0$. Where $y \ge \nu$, $s_\nu = s \le 1$, since $|f-y| \le f+y$. Where $y < \nu$: if $f \ge y$, $|f-y| = f-y \le f < f+\nu$; if $f < y$, $|f-y| = y-f \le y < \nu \le f+\nu$. So $s_\nu < 1$ there. (by S2; algebra)
  S4 (c), no jump at zero. The denominator $f+\max\{y,\nu\}$ is at least $\nu > 0$ for every $f \ge 0$, so $s_\nu(y,f)$ is a continuous function of $f$ on $f \ge 0$, at $f = 0$ too. Since $0 \le s_\nu \le 1$ (step 3), the expected term is continuous in $f$ as well, by bounded convergence. The sMAPE term of a zero actual is 0 at $f = 0$ and 1 for $f > 0$; so when zero actuals have probability $p_0 > 0$, the expected sMAPE term jumps by $p_0$ at $f = 0$. (by S3; bounded convergence)
  S5 (d). A count is 0 or at least 1. With $\nu \le 1$, a count of at least 1 is at least $\nu$, so $s_\nu = s$ by step 1. A zero count is below $\nu$, where $s_\nu(0,f) = f/(f+\nu)$ and $s(0,f) = 1$ for $f > 0$ (step 2). (by S1, S2; algebra)
  S6 So the msMAPE term is the sMAPE term for actuals of at least $\nu$. Below $\nu$ its denominator no longer shrinks with the actual: a zero actual scores $f/(f+\nu)$, and the expected msMAPE has no jump at the zero forecast. (by S5; conclusion)

L3 [sampling; affects C14, C15, C21; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The test sets are independent draws from a fixed distribution. L5 [model_specification; affects C6; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] C6 is proved for two groups that mirror each other about the median on the log scale. It is not proved for groups of different shape or spread.
L6 [measurement; affects C20, C21; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The definition of msMAPE is taken from Kolassa (2026, Definition 4). Smyl's article was not read. Smyl gives no value for the constant; ν = 0.1, 0.5 and 1 are this paper's choices.
L7 [other; affects C19; reduces_transferability; mitigation partial] That the relative efficiency of finite test sets tends to the asymptotic value is not shown; the simulation of C15 agrees with it at n = 10000.

# Qualitative studies

Version 0.1 (draft, to be reviewed by someone who works in the field) · 2026-11-13

For studies of text, talk or observation: interviews, focus groups, documents, online posts, field notes. In a
claimset lab, coded material is recorded with the qualitative extension (codes, codings and counts).

## Principle

A qualitative conclusion rests on what material was gathered, how it was read and coded, and by whom. Those choices
are made before the conclusion, and a reader can judge the conclusion only when they are stated: which approach
(themes found in the material, or a frame applied to it), how cases were chosen and when gathering stopped, how
codes were developed and checked, and where the researcher stands.

## approach

- **When:** any qualitative analysis.
- **Default:** name the approach and follow it as its authors describe: for example reflexive thematic analysis,
  codebook thematic analysis, or content analysis that counts coded material. Say whether codes come from the
  material (inductive) or from a frame fixed beforehand (deductive).
- **Why:** quality means different things in different approaches; judging one by another's standard misleads
  (Braun and Clarke 2021).
- **References:** Braun and Clarke 2006; Braun and Clarke 2021.

## sampling-and-saturation

- **When:** choosing how many and which cases (people, documents, posts).
- **Default:** state how cases are chosen and the rule for stopping, fixed in advance where the design allows (in a
  claimset lab, in the plan): for example, until a stated number of further cases adds no new code.
- **Why:** "until saturation" without a stated rule cannot be checked; empirical studies give typical numbers for
  given aims (Guest, Bunce and Johnson 2006; Hennink and Kaiser 2022).
- **References:** Guest, Bunce and Johnson 2006; Hennink and Kaiser 2022.

## second-coder

- **When:** codes are applied by a frame (codebook or content analysis), or counts decide a claim.
- **Default:** have a second coder code a sample independently, report agreement (Krippendorff's alpha) and how
  disagreements were resolved.
- **Why:** agreement shows that the codes can be applied consistently; in reflexive approaches, where the
  researcher's reading is the method, agreement measures do not fit, and reflexivity is reported instead (O'Connor
  and Joffe 2020; Braun and Clarke 2021).
- **References:** Hayes and Krippendorff 2007; O'Connor and Joffe 2020.

## counts

- **When:** a claim rests on how often something occurs in coded material.
- **Default:** fix in advance what is counted (the codes, the unit, distinct by what) and the rule that decides the
  claim (in a claimset lab, the plan's counts); report the denominator.
- **Why:** counts chosen after reading the material repeat what the reading already found.

## reporting

- **Default:** follow COREQ for interviews and focus groups, or SRQR for qualitative research in general.
- **References:** Tong, Sainsbury and Craig 2007; O'Brien et al. 2014.

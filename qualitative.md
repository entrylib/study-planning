# Qualitative studies

Version 0.2 (draft, to be reviewed by someone who works in the field) · 2026-11-13

For studies of text, talk or observation: interviews, focus groups, documents, online posts, field notes. In a
claimset lab, coded material is recorded with the qualitative extension (codes, codings and counts).

## Principle

A qualitative conclusion rests on what material was gathered, how it was read and coded, and by whom. Those choices
are made before the conclusion, and a reader can judge the conclusion only when they are stated: which approach
(themes found in the material, or a frame applied to it), how cases were chosen and when gathering stopped, how
codes were developed and checked, and where the researcher stands.

## approach

- **When:** any qualitative analysis.
- **Default:** name the approach and follow it as its authors describe: for example reflexive thematic analysis or
  codebook thematic analysis ([Braun and Clarke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa); [Braun and Clarke 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238)), or content analysis, whose summative form counts coded material
  ([Hsieh and Shannon 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687)). Say whether codes are developed mainly from the material (inductive) or from existing theory or a frame
  set beforehand (deductive), and where the analysis lies between the two.
- **Why:** quality criteria differ between approaches, even between kinds of thematic analysis; judging one by
  another's standard misleads ([Braun and Clarke 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238)).
- **References:** [Braun and Clarke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa); [Braun and Clarke 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238); [Hsieh and Shannon 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687).

## sampling-and-saturation

- **When:** choosing how many and which cases (people, documents, posts).
- **Default:** state how cases are chosen and the rule for stopping, fixed in advance where the design allows (in a
  claimset lab, in the plan): for example, analyse an initial set of cases, then stop when a stated number of
  further cases adds no new code ([Francis et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440903194015); [Guest, Namey and Chen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232076)).
- **Why:** "until saturation" without a stated rule cannot be checked ([Francis et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440903194015)). Empirical studies give typical numbers,
  but only for given aims and populations: saturation came within 9–17 interviews or 4–8 focus groups in studies
  of relatively homogeneous populations with narrow aims ([Hennink and Kaiser 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2021.114523); [Guest, Bunce and Johnson 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/1525822X05279903)), and more are needed for wider ones.
- **References:** [Francis et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440903194015); [Guest, Namey and Chen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232076); [Guest, Bunce and Johnson 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/1525822X05279903); [Hennink and Kaiser 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2021.114523).

## second-coder

- **When:** codes are applied by a frame (codebook or content analysis), or counts decide a claim.
- **Default:** have a second coder code a share of the material independently (10–25% of units is usual), report
  agreement (Krippendorff's alpha, per code where codes can overlap) and how disagreements were resolved
  ([O'Connor and Joffe 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406919899220); [Hayes and Krippendorff 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/19312450709336664)).
- **Why:** agreement shows that a coding frame can be applied consistently ([O'Connor and Joffe 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406919899220)). In reflexive thematic analysis,
  where the researcher's reading is the method, agreement measures do not fit, and the researcher's reflexive
  engagement is reported instead ([Braun and Clarke 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238)).
- **References:** [Hayes and Krippendorff 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/19312450709336664); [O'Connor and Joffe 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406919899220); [Braun and Clarke 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238).

## counts

- **When:** a claim rests on how often something occurs in coded material.
- **Default:** fix in advance what is counted (the codes, the unit, distinct by what) and the rule that decides the
  claim (in a claimset lab, the plan's counts); report the denominator.
- **Why:** counts chosen after reading the material describe what the reading found but cannot test it, because the
  reading chose them; a test is defined before the outcomes are seen ([Nosek et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)).
- **References:** [Nosek et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114).

## reporting

- **Default:** follow COREQ for interviews and focus groups ([Tong, Sainsbury and Craig 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzm042)), or SRQR for qualitative research in general,
  including documents and observation ([O'Brien et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000000388)). Both ask for the research team's characteristics and reflexivity.
- **References:** [Tong, Sainsbury and Craig 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzm042); [O'Brien et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000000388).
